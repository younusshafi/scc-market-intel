# 1
Our opinion
In our opinion, the financial statements of Galfar Engineering and Contracting SAOG (the “Parent Company”) and the consolidated financial statements of the Parent Company and its subsidiaries (together the “Group”)
present fairly, in all material respects, the respective financial positions of the Parent Company and the Group as at 31 December 2025, and their respective financial performance and their respective cash flows for the year then
ended in accordance with IFRS Accounting Standards.
What we have audited
The financial statements of the Parent Company and the consolidated financial statements of the Group (together “these financial statements”) comprise their respective:
statement of financial position as at 31 December 2025;
statement of comprehensive income for the year then ended;
statement of changes in equity for the year then ended;
statement of cash flows for the year then ended; and
notes to these financial statements, comprising material accounting policy information and other explanatory information.
Basis for opinion
We conducted our audit in accordance with International Standards on Auditing (ISAs). Our responsibilities under those standards are further described in the Auditor’s responsibilities for the audit of these financial statements
section of our report.
We believe that the audit evidence we have obtained is sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for our opinion.
Independence
We are independent of the Group in accordance with the International Code of Ethics for Professional Accountants (including International Independence Standards) issued by the International Ethics Standards Board for
Accountants (IESBA Code) as applicable to audits of financial statements of public interest entities and the ethical requirements that are relevant to our audit of these financial statements in the Sultanate of Oman. We have
fulfilled our other ethical responsibilities in accordance with these requirements and the IESBA Code.

Dear Shareholders,
On behalf of the Board of Directors, I am pleased to present the financial performance report of Galfar Engineering & Contracting SAOG (“the Parent Company”) and its
subsidiaries and associated companies (collectively “the Consolidated Group”) for the period ended 31 December 2025.

During the 2025 financial year, Galfar continued to stabilize its operational performance while navigating a competitive market environment and project-specific execution
challenges. The year reflects gradual progress and recognized improvements in strengthening project delivery discipline, improving cost management practices, and enhancing
operational efficiency across the Group.
Revenues remained broadly stable during the year. The Parent Company recorded revenue of OMR 272.2 million compared with OMR 272.7 million in 2024, while
consolidated revenue stood at OMR 285.7 million compared with OMR 286.0 million in the previous year. This stability reflects the steady execution of the Group’s project
backlog and the continuity of business activity across its core sectors.
Operational profitability improved compared with the previous year. EBITDA increased to OMR 22.3 million at the Parent level (compared to OMR 10.2 million in 2024) and
to OMR 24.8 million at the Group level (compared to OMR 12.1 million in the prior year). Gross profit similarly increased to OMR 16.5 million for the Parent Company and
OMR 18.6 million for the Group, reflecting improved project margin realization, tighter site-level controls, and continued emphasis on operational efficiency.
The Group recorded a net profit after tax of OMR 5.20 million compared with a net loss of OMR 3.91 million in 2024. At the Parent Company level, net profit after tax
amounted to OMR 4.78 million compared with a loss of OMR 3.67 million in the previous year. This improvement reflects stronger execution discipline and ongoing cost
optimization across projects. The results were achieved despite the impact of prudent provisioning for expected credit losses under IFRS 9 and exceptional legal costs
associated with ongoing international arbitration matters.
During the year, the Group also continued progressing in major and complex regional infrastructure projects, including the UAE–Oman Railway Link Project (Abu Dhabi –
Sohar) through the NNGT Joint Venture, which represents an important strategic infrastructure development for the region. In addition, the Group continued strong presence
and execution of critical projects in Oil & Gas, Roads, Utilities, Health and Urban Development sectors.

Subsidiaries continued to align with the Group’s operational recovery efforts. Aspire Readymix and Al Khalij Heavy Equipment together generated profits of approximately

Services has undergone targeted restructuring positioning it well for improved performance in 2026 and beyond.
Although the company did not bag any new project awards in 2025, it ended the year with an order backlog of approximately OMR 745 million, providing reasonable
visibility for future revenue streams. The backlog remains largely concentrated in the oil & gas, infrastructure, and utilities sectors, which continue to represent the core markets
for the Group.
Outlook for the Future:
Galfar enters 2026 with a more stable operational platform supported by a healthy order backlog, improved execution discipline, and a continued focus on strengthening cash
flow and project profitability.
Macroeconomic conditions in Oman remain supportive of activity in the engineering and construction sector. The national budget continues to prioritize investments in
infrastructure, transportation, water networks, and energy diversification. These sectors remain closely aligned with Galfar’s technical capabilities and project experience.
In addition, investments in oil & gas, petrochemicals, renewable energy, and Public-Private Partnership (PPP) initiatives are expected to generate opportunities for experienced
national contractors. Galfar will continue to pursue such opportunities selectively, focusing on projects where technical capability, execution capacity, and risk management align
with the Group’s operational objectives.
Regionally, infrastructure and industrial diversification programs across GCC countries continue to create additional prospects. While the competitive landscape remains
challenging, Galfar’s technical track record, operating scale, and long-standing presence in the market position the Group to participate selectively in regional opportunities
where appropriate.
Looking ahead, the Company will maintain its focus on disciplined project selection, operational efficiency, and digitalization of project management systems. The Group will
also continue exploring relevant opportunities in various sectors where these activities complement its core EPC capabilities and align with Oman Vision 2040.
Our people remain central to the Company’s long-term development. With more than 4,800 Omani employees across the organization, Galfar continues to play a leading
significant role in national employment and skills development. We take pride in our position as the largest employer of Omanis in the private sector, and in our commitment to
investing in training and leadership development to ensure that the Company’s workforce remains capable of supporting future growth.
With a healthy backlog, continued cost discipline, and improving operational performance, the Group is cautiously optimistic about sustaining progress in the coming period
while maintaining a prudent and disciplined approach to project execution.
At the same time, the Group continues to monitor the evolving regional geopolitical environment and the potential economic implications arising from ongoing conflicts in the
wider region. While the direct impact on the Company’s current operations has been limited to date, prolonged instability could influence regional economic conditions, supply
chain dynamics, material costs, and the timing of project awards. Management remains attentive to these developments and continues to maintain prudent operational planning,
diversified sourcing strategies, and disciplined risk management practices to mitigate potential disruptions and ensure the resilience of the Company’s operations.
Gratitude & Appreciation
On behalf of the Board of Directors, we extend our sincere appreciation to the Government of the Sultanate of Oman under the wise leadership of His Majesty Sultan Haitham
bin Tarik — may Allah protect him — for the continued efforts to support the nation’s economic development and infrastructure advancement.
For more than five decades, Galfar has contributed to the development of Oman’s infrastructure and industrial growth, and we remain committed to supporting the national
development agenda and the objectives of Oman Vision 2040.

GALFAR ENGINEERING & CONTRACTING
General Overview
During 2025, Oman’s construction sector continued to operate within a disciplined economic environment shaped by fiscal prudence and the long-term development
objectives of Oman Vision 2040. The sector’s growth increasingly reflects value-driven investment rather than volume expansion, with greater emphasis on capital efficiency,
balanced risk allocation, and sustainable project delivery.
Stable hydrocarbon revenues and improving fiscal indicators supported continued public investment across priority sectors including infrastructure, renewable energy, electricity
transmission and distribution, water and wastewater networks, logistics corridors, industrial zones, and integrated urban developments. These investments continue to position
construction as a key enabler of economic diversification.
At the same time, the sector is gradually transitioning as Oman advances its energy transition agenda, with increasing investment in utility-scale solar and wind projects, grid
reinforcement, and decarbonization-related infrastructure. This transition is increasing technical complexity and compliance requirements, creating opportunities for contractors
with strong engineering capabilities and integrated project delivery systems.
Despite these supportive fundamentals, the sector remains highly competitive and margin-sensitive. Pricing pressures, evolving procurement models, material cost volatility,
extended approval cycles, and strengthened ESG and In-Country Value (ICV) requirements continue to influence profitability and working capital dynamics.
Performance Overview – 2025
For the year ended 31 December 2025, the Group recorded consolidated revenue of approximately RO 286 million and reported a net profit after tax of RO 5.2 million
compared with a net loss of RO (3.9) million in 2024.
The Parent Company recorded revenue of RO 272 million (2024: RO 273 million) and achieved a net profit after tax of RO 4.8 million compared with a loss of RO (3.6)
million in the previous year. The improvement reflects stronger project execution discipline, improved cost control, and continued focus on operational efficiency across the
Group.
Subsidiary Performance
Galfar Aspire Ready-mix SPC recorded revenue of RO 16.6 million in 2025 (2024: RO 16.1 million) and generated a profit after tax of RO 0.5 million (2024: RO 0.6
million).
Aspire Projects and Services SPC recorded revenue of RO 6.1 million (2024: RO 4.4 million) and a net loss of RO (0.3) million, reflecting improvement compared with the
loss of RO (0.8) million recorded in 2024.
Al Khalij Heavy Equipment & Engineering LLC reported revenue of RO 2.3 million (2024: RO 3.0 million) and achieved a marginal profit of RO 0.01 million compared with a
loss of RO (0.1) million in the previous year.
Market Position and Order Book
Following the strong order intake of RO 616 million in 2024, management adopted a calibrated bidding strategy in 2025 focused on protecting margins, maintaining balanced
contractual risk exposure, and aligning project commitments with execution capacity and cashflow considerations.
Order inflow during the year comprised approximately RO 9 million in newly secured contracts and RO 33 million in variations and additional works on existing projects. The
moderated intake reflects extended evaluation cycles for major tenders, project deferments, and selective participation aligned with defined return thresholds and risk
management principles.
In certain cases, the Company elected not to participate in tenders where commercial terms or project structures were not aligned with its risk framework. Competitive
intensity remained elevated across several segments, particularly within power and utilities, where aggressive pricing from regional contractors influenced award outcomes.

The Company closed the year with an order backlog of approximately RO 745 million, providing revenue visibility and operational continuity.
Over the five-year period from 2021 to 2025, the Company maintained an estimated market share in the range of 15–17% with a value-weighted tender success rate

Strategic Positioning
The Company continues to advance its transformation agenda across operational efficiency, diversification, digital integration, and sustainability alignment.
In line with Oman Vision 2040 and the national energy transition strategy, Galfar is selectively expanding its capabilities in high-voltage substations, renewable energy projects,
environmental infrastructure, and PPP delivery frameworks. Investments in digital project controls and strategic partnerships are being strengthened to enhance competitiveness
in specialized EPC segments.
The operating model continues to evolve beyond traditional construction toward integrated infrastructure solutions supporting energy transition, industrial modernization, and
long-term economic diversification.
Sustainability, In-Country Value and Corporate Responsibility
Pathway to Net Zero
In FY2025, total greenhouse gas emissions decreased by approximately 18.3% compared with FY2024, with Scope 1 emissions declining by 18.0% and Scope 2 emissions
decreasing by 21.4%. These reductions were primarily driven by fuel optimization and strengthened energy management controls.
Climate considerations remain integrated into operational oversight and enterprise risk management processes, supporting alignment with Oman’s Net Zero 2050 target.
In-Country Value (ICV)
During FY2025, total ICV expenditure on locally manufactured goods and services amounted to RO 54.45 million, including RO 16.2 million directed to small and medium
enterprises (SMEs).
Workforce nationalization and capability development remain key pillars of the Company’s ICV framework through structured training programs, leadership development
initiatives, and knowledge transfer mechanisms.
Corporate Social Responsibility
The Company maintained a structured CSR program focused on healthcare, education, community welfare, and infrastructure support initiatives. Activities included support for
healthcare infrastructure, educational sponsorship programs, and community engagement initiatives across several wilayats.
Quality, Health, Safety and Environment (QHSE)
Galfar continues to maintain high standards of quality, health, safety, and environmental management through integrated systems aligned with ISO 9001:2015, ISO
45001:2018, ISO 14001:2015, and ISO/TS 29001:2010 standards.
Operational safety performance remained strong in 2025 despite increased project activity. The Company achieved a Lost Time Injury Frequency (LTIF) of 0.06 and a Total
Recordable Case Frequency (TRCF) of 1.24, both significantly below industry benchmarks.
The Company also received recognition from major clients including PDO, OXY, and OQEP for safety performance and operational excellence.

Human Capital
Human capital development remains central to the Company’s long-term strategy. During 2025, the Group expanded its workforce significantly and continued to strengthen its

Outlook for 2026
As Oman implements the 2026 national budget within the current Five-Year Development Plan, infrastructure modernization, energy transition, logistics expansion, and

competitiveness.

The construction sector continues to operate within a dynamic risk environment influenced by competitive pricing pressures, evolving procurement models, cost volatility,
supply chain variability, and extended client approval cycles.
The Company continues to strengthen its enterprise risk management framework through enhanced tender-stage risk assessments, structured bid review processes, and digital
cost validation tools to support disciplined project selection and balanced contractual risk allocation.
